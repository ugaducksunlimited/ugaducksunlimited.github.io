Wetlands don't fill themselves back in.

We're the University of Georgia's student chapter of Ducks Unlimited — a group of students working to conserve wetlands, restore waterfowl habitat, and get more people outside learning why it matters.

Conserve

We work on habitat restoration and cleanup projects around Georgia's wetlands and waterways, in partnership with local Ducks Unlimited chapters.

Educate

Guest speakers, workshops, and hands-on sessions on waterfowl biology, wetland ecology, and conservation policy — open to any major.

Connect

A community of students who like the outdoors — from wildlife majors to first-timers who've never set foot in a marsh.

Part of a national mission, built at UGA

Ducks Unlimited got its start in 1937, when a small group of waterfowl hunters watched drought and habitat loss push North America's duck populations to historic lows during the Dust Bowl — and decided to do something about it. Nearly 90 years later, it's grown into the world's largest and most effective private wetlands and waterfowl conservation organization, working through partnerships with landowners, agencies, and scientists across the continent. Our chapter brings that same mission to campus, giving UGA students a way to get involved locally while they're still in school.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to what people usually ask before their first meeting.

Do I need hunting or biology experience to join?

Not at all. Members come from every major and background — some have never been in a duck blind, others grew up hunting. Curiosity is the only requirement.

How much are membership dues?

There's a small membership fee that supports chapter operations and conservation fundraising. The exact amount is announced each semester at meetings and on our social media.

When and where do you meet?

General meetings are every other week during the school year at 6 PM at the fire pit. Dates are posted each semester on our Instagram and Facebook.

Do you organize hunting trips?

Our chapter's focus is conservation, education, and community, so organized hunts vary year to year. Ask an officer at a meeting about what's planned this semester.

How do I stay updated on events?

Follow us on Instagram at @ugaducksunlimited — that's where we post exact dates, times, and locations as each semester's schedule firms up.

How do I become an officer?

Officer elections are held each spring and are open to any member in good standing. Shadowing a current officer beforehand is a great way to get a feel for the role.

About Ducks Unlimited

Ducks Unlimited began in 1937, during the Dust Bowl, when waterfowl populations had fallen to unprecedented lows and a small group of sportsmen decided to act. It is now the world's largest and most effective private waterfowl and wetlands conservation organization.

DU is a volunteer-based, grassroots organization working across the United States, Canada, and Mexico. Its conservation work runs through partnerships with private landowners, government agencies, and the scientific community — restoring habitat that supports waterfowl, countless other species, and people downstream.

Ducks Unlimited, Inc. is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit and holds the Platinum Seal of Transparency from Candid.

Mission & vision

Mission

Conserve, restore, and manage wetlands and associated habitats for North America's waterfowl.

Vision

Wetlands sufficient to fill the skies with waterfowl today, tomorrow, and forever.

Waterfowl & wetlands first

Habitat for North America's waterfowl drives every decision.

Science-based

Conservation guided by research and evaluation, not guesswork.

Collaboration

Partnership with landowners and agencies over confrontation.

Waterfowling heritage

The sport and tradition of waterfowl hunting, valued and carried forward.

Wise use of resources

At least 80% of resources go directly to the conservation mission.

Volunteer-driven

A grassroots organization powered by members and volunteers.
